Valve’s VR “Steam Frame” weighs half as much, priced from $1,059 (translated)

Player agency: how policy constrains it, how the industry shapes it, how narrative design realizes it.

인벤 Inven（韓國，韓語） · 2026.09.15Original language: 한국어

With Steam Frame, Valve is moving VR devices beyond being mere head-mounted hardware toward a SteamOS platform that can stream games and also run them on the device: in an ARM environment, it uses emulation and Proton to support x86 games, while streaming covers both VR and regular Steam games. The device weighs 440 grams including the head strap, and the 256GB and 1TB versions are priced at $1,059 and $1,299, respectively; alongside the lighter design, the price and lottery-based reservation create another barrier, and South Korea is not included in the initial sales. This makes Valve’s approach a simultaneous test of wearing burden, game compatibility, and players’ willingness to accept expensive hardware.

Lower weight means a lower barrier to wearing it for long periods; the price and lottery eligibility, however, push that barrier back to the point of purchase. For players, this is not simply a spec upgrade—it means weighing lightness, streaming convenience, and the satisfaction of owning the hardware, and deciding which is worth trading their own time and money for.
