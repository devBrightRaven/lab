The Switch 2 version of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time will respond to humming and instrument sounds, letting Link play the Ocarina of Time (translated)

Player agency: how policy constrains it, how the industry shapes it, how narrative design realizes it.

Inside Games · 2026.09.09Original language: 日本語

The Switch 2 version of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time has announced a feature that directly incorporates players’ voices into gameplay: Link will not only respond to button inputs on the controller, but can also trigger the game’s playing effects when players hum or even play the game-themed ocarina scheduled for release. This design turns an instrument originally played by the character into a medium for interaction between the player and the game; after the news was revealed, fans began discussing whether they could try other instruments, while some worried about their own pitch. The game is scheduled to launch on November 5, 2026.

Sakigarasu's Take

This design brings the player’s voice in from the outside world, adding another form of expression rather than another main storyline. It gives people familiar with the game a reason to play it again, and reminds us that player agency does not come only from branching endings; it also comes from whether a game is willing to respond to something you choose to do on your own.
