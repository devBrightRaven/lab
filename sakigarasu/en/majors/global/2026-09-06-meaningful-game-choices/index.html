3 Games That Truly Give Weight to Player Choices (translated)

Player agency: how policy constrains it, how the industry shapes it, how narrative design realizes it.

Polygon · 2026.09.06Original language: English

The article revisits what it means for choices to matter in games, arguing that although large branching structures and multiple endings create a strong sense of impact, the format has gradually become formulaic. It therefore introduces three works that respond to player decisions in different ways. In Zero Parades: For Dead Spies, character abilities affect dialogue checks and the way players understand events; even seemingly fatal decisions usually leave a way forward. In Slay the Princess, refusing to enter the cabin also counts as a valid choice and opens a new story branch. Last Defense Academy has 100 endings, and none of them is treated as canon.

Sakigarasu's Take

By moving choice away from the number of endings and toward concrete consequences in the world, these games give players more room to understand their own decisions.
